## Abstract
Social scientists often explain religious effects in terms of religious group affiliations. Typically, researchers identify religious groups by denomination or some broader popular categorization, such as “fundamentalist” or “evangelical.” To capture religious differences more effectively, Steensland et al. (2000) propose an intricate classification of American denominations that takes into account the theology and historical development of various American religious traditions to predict individual attitudes and behaviors. We believe that equal care and attention should be devoted to the development of key measures of belief that may cross denominational lines. In this article, we propose one such measure: personal conceptions or images of God. Our simple measure of conceptions of God predicts church attendance rates, belief in biblical literalism, political party identification, attitudes toward abortion, and attitudes about sexual morality. In addition, this indicator provides a means to understand variation within religious traditions. Views of God's character provide a straightforward way to describe religious differences and an efficient means to demonstrate how religion affects the world.
## Research Questions + Results
1. Does one's beliefs about God affect your attitudes and beliefs in other domains even when controlling for religious denomination? **Yes**.
2. Does significant heterogeneity exist within religious denominations regarding the nature of one's belief in God? **Yes**. While different religions have different mean levels of beliefs in regards to survey questions, all religious denominations exhibit significant variance in beliefs. Furthermore, estimating stratified models by religious denomination (only had sample size large enough for Mainline Protestants) revealed that the nature of one's belief in God was significantly associated with the outcome variables.
## Independent variable
1. **The nature of one's belief in God**: Items were [[Variable standardization|standardized]] and summed to form a scale. [[Cronbach's Alpha]] of 0.62 (a little low).
	1. Is God more like a mother or a father? (7-point scale).
	2. Is God more like a master or a spouse? (7-point scale).
	3. Is God more like a judge or a lover? (7-point scale).
	4. Is God more like a friend or a king? (7-point scale).
	5. To me, life is meaningful only because God exists.
	6. God who concerns himself with every human being personally.
## Dependent variables
1. **Church attendance**: 9-point scale.
2. **Biblical literalism**.
3. **Abortion Attitudes**. Set of 7 yes/no questions as to under what circumstances abortion would be acceptable which were summed. [[Cronbach's Alpha]] of 0.9.
4. **Sexual Attitudes**. Set of 3 questions using a 4-point scale as to how acceptable homosexuality, premarital sex, and extramarital sex are. [[Cronbach's Alpha]] of 0.6 (which is a little low).
5. **Political Affiliation**. 7-point scale.
## Models
1. [[Ordinary Least Squares Regression]] for every outcome.
