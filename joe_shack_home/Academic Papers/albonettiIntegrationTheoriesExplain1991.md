## Abstract

Based on an integration of work on uncertainty avoidance in decision making with research on causal attribution in punishment, the author hypothesizes that judges attempt to manage uncertainty by developing "patterned responses" that are the product of an attribution process involving assessments of the offender's likelihood of committing future crime. Washington, D. C., felony sentencing data generated by the Prosecutor's Management and Information System (PROMIS) were used to test this integrated theoretical model. Support for the theoretical integration is provided by the evidence of the effects of prior record, defendant's race, use of a weapon, pretrial release, and the interaction between defendant's race and bail outcome on sentence severity. Contrary to common suppositions, information on defendant-victim relationship and victim provocation was unrelated to sentence severity. Further research should examine judges' attempt to reduce uncertainty by relying on stereotypes and attributions linked to the likelihood of recidivism.

**Abstract**: Judges attempt to manage uncertainty by developing patterned responses that are the product of an attribution process involving assessments of the offender’s likelihood of committing future crime. Contrary to common belief, information on defendant-victim relationship and victim provocation were unrelated to sentence severity.

**Theory**
- **Structural organizational approach** (rational choice models of decision making are useful points of departure for understanding the exercise of discretion) -> complete knowledge eliminates uncertainty in decision making, absent that actors reduce uncertainty by relying upon a rationality that is the product of habit and social structure. 
	- Established operating procedure, division of labor, hierarchy, formal channel of communication, professional training, and indoctrination absorb uncertainty resulting from incomplete knowledge -> “bounded rationality” -> decision making made on the basis of past experience, stereotypes, prejudices -> achieve a measure of rationality by developing patterned responses which reduce uncertainty -> satisficing.
	- Information presented to judges which demonstrates increased likelihood of recidivating is thought to increase severity of sanction. Opposite for decreased likelihood.
	- **Summary**: Uncertainty surrounding sentencing arises from an inability to predict future criminal behavior. Using defendant characteristics, circumstances of the crime, and case processing outcomes, judges assess the defendant’s disposition toward future criminal activity. Attributions (from the judge) that you are a criminal will increase sentence severity. Attributions that this was a temporary thing will decrease sentence severity.

**Data and Methods**: 2158 felony cases processed in the Superior Court of Washington DC in 1974. Use a probit model to estimate the probability of conviction. Then they use an OLS regression where the dependent variable is sentence severity.
- **Prior Record of Felony Convictions**: Hypothesized to increase sentence severity -> you have a stable criminal dispositional character -> likely to commit future crime.
- **Offense Type**: Seven-category variable with each category reflecting a level of harm (none, potential, real) with the object of harm (person, property).
- **Defendant Race**: African-Americans stereotyped as being more dangerous.
- **Sex**: Females are believed to have lower probability of future criminal behavior.
- **Use of a weapon**: Indicative of a stable criminal dispositional character -> likely to commit future crime.
- **Defendant-Victim Relationship**: Crimes against intimates are judged not to be indicative of a criminal character, but crimes against strangers will be judged as indicative of a criminal character.
- **Victim Provoked the Offense**: Reduce severity.
- **Pretrial Release Outcome**: Those who get bail set (whether or not they can post it) will be sentenced more harshly.
- **Plea**: Offenders pleading guilty will receive less severe sanctions than comparable offenders insisting on a trial.
- **Statutory Severity**: Control variable in the analysis. It’s a legally relevant variable in determining sentence outcome.  

**Findings**
- Black social status, pretrial release outcome, weapon use, prior record of felonies are associated with higher sentence severity.    
	- Add a race, bail interaction term indicating sentence severity is much worse for African-Americans who get bail set vs. White-Americans who get bail set.
- Pleading guilty is associated with lesser sentence severity.    
- Defendant/victim relationship and case information indicating the victim provoked the offense are unrelated to sentence severity (in the predicted direction, not significant).
- Concerned with estimates of sentence severity (not so much on whether or not a defendant is convicted or not). Not much discussion on this aspect as a result.

Attribution Theory -> In groups and out groups. When faced with rule breaking or disapproved of behavior, if it’s a member of an internal group we attribute the wrongdoing to something external to that person (sick, stressed, bad day) vs. if it’s a member of an out-group we attribute the wrongdoing to something internal to that person. **Fundamental Attribution Error**.
