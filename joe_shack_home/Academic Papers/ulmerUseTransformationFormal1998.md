## Abstract

Our study focuses on the use and transformation of sentencing guidelines in interorganizational relations. We draw on the organizational concepts of "embeddedness" (R. Perrucci, 1994) and "properties in use" (M. Peyrot, 1995) of formal decision-making tools to frame our analysis. We present qualitative interview and field data on organizational contexts and case processing strategies from three different-sized county trial courts in Pennsylvania, a state whose courts have operated under sentencing guidelines for over a decade. Among the highlights of the analysis are our findings that DA's offices can use sentencing guidelines as management tools, judges can use them to legitimate their sentencing practices in political disputes, and both prosecutors and defense attorneys use them as important tools of uncertainty reduction in their guilty plea strategies. More generally, we argue that the implementation of externally imposed formal rules and decision-making criteria depends on local relationships, activities, and informal decision-making criteria. - **What is the research question?** → How do formal sentencing guidelines interact with workgroup case processing strategies in local courts?
   -- 
- **Why is it important?** → Contexts of local court communities and the formal/informal case processing norms of courtroom workgroups are at least as important as formal laws and state-level policies in determining the contours of criminal justice.
  
	- Formal properties (what was intended) vs. properties in use (how guidelines are actually used). Sentencing guidelines’ properties in use are embedded in local contextual arrangements.
	- **Processual Order Theory** → Actors with varying interests, ideologies, and commitments confront problematic situations. They continuously engage in interaction strategies (negotiation, cooperation, manipulation) to solve the problems. The outcomes of these interaction processes become sedimented into social orders (informal norms and routines), but they can change or be reinforced through interaction processes. This ordering is embedded in larger sets of contextual conditions which constrain and enable participant interaction.
	- **Court Community Framework** → Courts are localized, diverse processual orders in which case processing and sentencing practices develop through the ongoing interaction of courtroom workgroup members (which is contextualized by local court interorganizational relationships and state policies).
	- **Important contextual features of courts (influence effect of guidelines)** → 1) Membership stability, 2) Local party politics, 3) Interorganizational relations and balances of power between sponsoring agencies, 4) Dominant sentencing ideologies and goals, 5) Organizational type and leadership style of prosecutor, 6) Strength and nature of going rates.
	  
- **How do you propose to answer it?** → Qualitative interview and field data on organizational contexts and case processing strategies from three different-sized county trial courts in PA where sentencing guidelines have been in effect for over a decade.
  
- **What do you find?**
  
	- Local courtrooms filter externally imposed formal policies and rules through local organizational cultures and strategic interaction processes.
	- DA’s offices use sentencing guidelines as management tools. 
	- Prosecutors and defense attorneys use them as tools of uncertainty reduction in guilty plea negotiations.
	- The effects of externally imposed formal rules depend on local relationships, activities, and informal decision-making criteria (i.e the processual order).
	- The effect of the guidelines depends on the local court context in which they are applied (e.g. defense + judge team-up, ignore them, increased prosecutorial control over ADAs, augment prosecutorial power).
