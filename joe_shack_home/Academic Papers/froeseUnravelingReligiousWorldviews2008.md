## Abstract
Not only do few studies address the issue of how religious belief relates to political ideology, but little attempt has also been made to analyze this relationship from a comparative perspective. Using data from the International Social Survey Program, we examine how images of God, as measured by God's perceived level of engagement and authority, relate to political ideology in seven Western industrial and postindustrial societies. We find that variation in images of God has no effect on whether individuals are politically liberal or conservative in five of seven countries. Nonetheless, beliefs about God are strongly related to abortion and sexual morality attitudes in every country, but only sporadically related to ideas about social and economic justice. In the end, we argue that theological beliefs tend to be unrelated to a general measure of political ideology, not because religious beliefs are politically unimportant in these societies, but rather because religious perspectives are rarely fully liberal or conservative in their political orientation. In addition, we find that Americans hold unique views of God in comparison to other countries in our sample and that the American tendency to view God as more active and authoritative affects policy attitudes in ways contrary to the effects of church attendance.
## Theory and Background
1. Why is being religious connected to being politically conservative in the modern-day? Especially since religiosity has inspired liberal and leftist movements in the past.
2. Authors reject the argument that what binds religiosity with conservatism is the emphasis on individualism and restraining government influence over society.
3. Want to conduct a cross-national comparison using the nature of one's belief in God to understand: 1) how different conceptions of God influences social/moral beliefs, and 2) if this relationship holds across all nations.
## Hypotheses
1. Individuals who believe in a more engaged and authoritative God will also believe their God offers clear distinctions between good and evil. As a result, these individuals will be more inclined to have punitive attitudes towards behaviors deemed sinful and which go against traditional values e.g., abortion and homosexuality.
2. Individuals who believe in a more active and authoritarian God will favor policies which advocate for economic individualism. The idea being that those who believe in an active and authoritative God will view economic hardship and success as God-given and will be less likely to support legislation which are contrary to God's wishes.
## Independent Variables
All survey items were [[Variable standardization|standardized]] and summed to form the scales. They also conducted a nebulous [[Factor Analysis]] and found the items loaded onto the theorized latent constructs.

1. **How active and present is God in the world?** (5-point scale ranging from strongly agree to strongly disagree). [[Cronbach's Alpha]] of 0.84.
	1. There is a God who concerns himself with every human being personally.
	2. To me, life is meaningful only because God exists.
2. **What is the nature of God's personal traits?** (7-point scale). I may be getting the comparisons wrong. [[Cronbach's Alpha]] of 0.53 which is very low. They have a footnote explaining why they think it's still OK.
	1. King vs. Friend.
	2. Judge vs. Lover.
	3. Master vs. Spouse.
	4. Father vs. Mother.
3. Interaction of the two.
## Dependent variables
1. **Political Ideology**: 5-point scale ranging from far left to far right.
2. **Abortion attitudes**: 4-point scale (Not at all wrong, Sometimes wrong, Almost always wrong, Always wrong). [[Cronbach's Alpha]] of 0.75 (although there are only two items).
	1. The extent to which abortion is wrong when the baby has a serious defect?
	2. The extent to which abortion is wrong when the family has a low income?
3. **Sexual morality scale**: 4-point scale (Not at all wrong, Sometimes wrong, Almost always wrong, Always wrong). [[Cronbach's Alpha]] of 0.64 which is a little low.
	1. The extent to which premarital sex is wrong?
	2. The extent to which adultery is wrong?
	3. The extent to which homosexuality is wrong?
4. **To what degree is the government responsible for providing jobs?**
5. **To what degree is it the government's job to reduce income differences?**

## Results
1. Views of God are not consistently associated with political ideology across countries (although the evidence is never in the direction of being associated with leftist ideologies).
2. Views of God are consistently associated with sexuality beliefs (more active conceptions of God and more authoritative conceptions of God are associated with more restrictive beliefs on sexuality).
3. Higher rates of church attendance and beliefs in an active God are associated with more restrictive attitudes towards abortion. Beliefs in an authoritative God are not very predictive.
4. View of God are not consistently associated with economic beliefs.
5.  Weak results, but the trend indicates that higher beliefs in an active God are more associated with traditionally leftist or liberal economic policies.

Why this pattern of results? Authors argue there is an unresolved tension between the beliefs of moral absolutism and the obligation to seek social justice. The more active you believe God to be, the more likely you are to condemn *deviant behavior* but also potentially the more likely you are to advocate for liberal/leftist economic policies. If you believe in strict religious moral codes but also egalitarian economic policies, you must choose between those values when voting. This causes religion to appear unrelated to political ideology.

5. Findings indicate the USA is somewhat of an outlier as we had the highest mean levels of belief in an active God and in an authoritative God. Furthermore, the measures had the highest correlation within the US.
6. USA-specific regression analyses revealed:
	1. Higher rates of Church attendance were associated with wanting lower levels of defense spending, higher levels of gun control, and lower levels of support for the death penalty.
	2. Meanwhile, the more active you viewed God, the more you supported higher levels of defense spending and less gun control. The authoritarian you viewed God, the more you favored the death penalty.

The nature of your belief in God (separate from your religious affiliation as well as the rate at which you attend services) has independent predictive power of your social and moral attitudes.
## Future work
1. Ask individuals if God is primarily concerned with individual-level morality or collective morality.
2. Better measures of how authoritative God seems as asking someone if God is more like a judge or a lover introduces a dichotomy that may not exist.
