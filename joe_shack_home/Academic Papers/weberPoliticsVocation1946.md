## Notes
1. A state is a human community that has successfully claimed the monopoly over the legitimate use of force within a given space.
2. Politics is the striving to share power or influence the distribution of power among states or within a state.
3. There are three types of legitimating domination:
	1. Tradition
	2. Charisma
	3. Belief in the validity of the rules and structures because you believe in the process which created them (i.e., it was rational).
4. Politically dominant powers maintain their power through material reward and social honor of their administrative staff.
5. States can be classified based on if the administrators own the administrative means of production or if the staff is separated from the means of production. An ancient example would be if during war you supplied your own weapons and soldiers of if your lord did. Under the modern state, the state controls all means of administration.
6. This has led to *professional politicians* which are men who are expert administrators and never aim to be the one fully in charge. Public functionaries are thus divided into the political leaders (who can and often do get replaced regularly through political processes) and administrative officials (who should apply technical expertise to run the state according to the desires of the political officials regardless of their own views).
	1. Modern bureaucracy has necessitated the hiring of individuals who are competent and not simply beneficiaries of the spoils system.
7. To make politics one's vocation, one must live *for* politics and/or one must live *off* politics. Meaning it gives you internal purpose and/or a means to survival. You usually must be somewhat wealthy or at least be able to to not have to spend the majority of your time looking for sustenance if politics is not to provide you much in the way of means of survival. Or you have a robust system compensation for people working in politics.
8. There are three main qualities which make a successful politician: 1) passion, 2) feeling of responsibility, and 3) a sense of proportion.
	1. Passion for a cause or a feeling of responsibility to a cause, that is. Without a cause, the politician begins to seek power for the sake of gaining power and becomes a bad politician. They can also lose their sense of responsibility and began to accrue power for themselves thus descending into vanity.
	2. The passion must be balanced with a sense of distance to allow for the realities of the world to guide their actions as to what is possible.
9. To be a good politician is to always hold in balance the means and ends and what ends justify which means. "If an action of good intent leads to bad results, then, in the actor's eyes, not he but the world, or the stupidity of other men, or God's will who made them thus, is responsible for the evil. However a man who believes in an ethic of responsibility takes account of precisely the average deficiencies of people... From no ethics in the world can it be concluded when and to what extent the ethically good purpose justifies the ethically dangerous means and ramifications."
