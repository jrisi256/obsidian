## Abstract

While pleading guilty has become ubiquitous in criminal trial courts, limited research has focused on the plea process and the factors that influence guilty plea convictions. Numerous theoretical accounts of the plea process highlight the importance of the court actors and their interactions. Based on this research, the current study analyzes the impact of courtroom actor familiarity and similarity on the chosen mode of disposition and the time to disposition. The findings demonstrate that similarity among the actors and familiarity between the prosecutor and judge increase the odds of a plea disposition and reduce the days to disposition. However, familiarity of the defense attorney seems to impede on the informal plea process, such that cases are more likely to proceed to trial when the defense attorney is more familiar with the other actors.

## Introduction

What factors influence guilty plea convictions? Similarity among courtroom actors and familiarity between judge and prosecutor increase the odds of a plea disposition and reduce the days to disposition. However familiarity of the defense attorney seems to impede on the formal plea process. Cases are more likely to proceed to trial when the defense attorney is more familiar with the other actors.
## Research Questions

**Big Question**: How does the similarity and familiarity of courtroom actors influence the odds of a guilty plea disposition and also the speed at which that decision is reached?

**Hypothesis**: Increased familiarity and similarity between actors should facilitate cooperation leading to an increased likelihood of a guilty plea and a shorter time to disposition. Shared experiences and characteristics should lead to agreement concerning goals and cooperation in achieving those goals, namely the reduction of uncertainty and increase in efficiency (processing of cases). Since trials are antithetical to these goals, guilty pleas become the preferred outcome.

**Theory**: Bureaucratic nature of courts aims to reduce uncertainty and increase efficiency. Trials are antithetical to both leading to the prioritization of guilty pleas. Prosecutors want to reduce workloads and maintain public image (keep convictions up). Defense attorneys also want to efficiently move their cases. Judges are concerned about their reputation, reduce caseload, and minimize decisions which can be overturned through appeal. All these factors come together in favor of cooperation despite what may be hypothesized to be an otherwise adversarial system.

## Data

- Data collected from public defender case files from a single circuit courthouse in a single county in Florida. 497 randomly sampled felony pleas and all 408 felony trials. Collected between 2002 - 2010.
- Courtroom assignments changed. Paired with prosecutors for a while, could randomly change. Worked regularly with a judge but turnover happened frequently. Almost always the same defense, prosecutor, and judge handled the case after arraignment during hearings and sentencing.
- Judges elected in nonpartisan elections for 6 year terms. State attorney and public defender run in partisan elections for 4 year terms. 66 different prosecutors, 118 different defense attorneys, and 36 different judges. Pretty liberal.
- Changes: Beginning in July 2001, responsibility for preparing guidelines was placed solely with the State Attorney’s Office. In 2005, penalties were enhanced for sexual crimes against children.

### Dependent Variables

- **Tendency to settle** is captured in mode of disposition. Plea disposition is coded such that cases disposed of by a guilty plea or no contest are 1 while cases disposed of through a jury/bench trial are 0.
- Speed of case processing is **time to disposition**. Total number of days from the day the defendant was arrested to the day they were declared guilty or not guilty. Lower values indicate greater efficiency.
### Independent Variables

- Measured for the full work group, the prosecutor and defense attorney pair, and prosecutor and judge pair.
- **Familiarity**:
	1) Continuous and reflects the number of interactions between the work group or pair.
	2) Dummy variable which indicates all three (or two) actors appeared together (1) or not (0) at least once before. Underestimation → using only a sample. Biased against finding an effect for familiarity.
- **Similarity**:
	- Law school similarity: In which all actors went to the same law school.
	- Race: All actors are the same race (1 = everyone is black or all non-Black, 0 otherwise).
	- Sex: All actors are the same sex.
	- Experience: Difference in years of experience for the actors.
	- All four combined into an index.
- **Controls**: Offense level, Prior Record, Number of counts (total number of counts against defendant in current case), Weapon Used, Probation/Parole violation, Detained pretrial, Race (unfortunately only Black/non-Black), Sex, Age, Dummy variables for year.
- **Analysis**: [[Multilevel modeling|hierarchical linear modeling]]. Cases are nested within work groups (and pairs). Allows for the intercepts to vary across work groups and pairs.
## Results

* **Descriptively...**
	* More familiar work groups tend to take their cases to trial.
	* Familiar prosecutors and judges seem to rely more on plea convictions.
	* More similar workgroups are more likely to plead guilty.
	* **Controls**
		- Cases with more serious offenses and more extensive priors were less likely to be resolved by plea.
		- Cases involving older, Black, males were less likely to result in a guilty plea.
		- Defendants on probation/parole and those who were detained pretrial had greater odds of being disposed of by a plea.
    
- **Key Independent Variables for Likelihood of Guilty Pleas (Full Work group)**
	- Greater familiarity among the work group decreased the odds of a plea.
	- Cases handled by a work group that was the same sex had greater odds of being resolved by a guilty plea.
	- Greater difference in experience decreased the odds of a guilty plea.
	- Law school and race didn’t have a significant impact. The index signals more dissimilar work groups are more likely to proceed to trial.

- **Key Independent Variables for Speed of Disposition (Full Work group)**
	- While familiarity decreased the time to disposition, it wasn’t significant.
	- Work groups of similar sex came to quicker resolutions. Generally speaking, more dissimilar work groups came to slower resolutions.
    
- **Key Independent Variables (Prosecutor and Defense)**
	- Same sex pairings lead to higher odds of guilty plea.
	- Differences in experience nor general dissimilarity led to decreased odds of guilty plea.
	- As a count, familiarity had no impact. As a dummy, familiarity is associated with lower odds of a guilty plea.
	- Familiarity led to increased speed of disposition. Sex similarity led to speedier resolutions. General dissimilarity didn’t have an effect.

- **Key Independent Variables (Prosecutor and Judge)**
	- No effect for sex, race, or law school on probability of a plea. Greater differences in experience led to lower odds of a plea. Generally dissimilar pairs led to lower odds of a guilty plea.
	- More familiar prosecutors and judges (as measured by the dummy NOT the count) actually led to more pleas.
	- No significant variables on speed to disposition.

### Results summed up

- Familiarity between the judge and prosecutor was associated with higher odds of a plea deal and shorter times to disposition (as measured by the dummy variable). This was not the case for defense attorneys. Familiarity either had no impact or led to a decrease in the odds of a plea deal.
- More dissimilar work groups generally had lower odds of reaching a plea deal and longer times to disposition. Same-sex work groups generally had higher odds of reaching a plea deal and shorter times to disposition.
### Interviews

Meant to add context. Small sample. All parties stressed the necessity of pleas and cooperation. Caseloads are too high. System cannot handle everything going to trial.

Some animosity between the defense and prosecutor offices regardless though. Differing philosophies really about their jobs. The plea process isn’t always so smooth. Defense attorneys don’t really have chummy relationships with the judges or prosecutors either. The relationship really exists only within the courtroom. Prosecutors and judges are more friendly.  
## Conclusions

- Similarity matters and in particular sex similarity and general dissimilarity.
- Increased participation of the defense attorney serves as a barrier against case processing. Familiarity between prosecutor and judge increased likelihood of a plea, this was not true when the familiarity of the defense attorney was taken into account. Not theoretically predicted. Why? Not sure. Maybe less familiar attorneys feel the need to go along. More familiar attorneys don’t feel this pressure. Shared pasts were ones of ideological conflict -> so more familiarity doesn’t lead to cohesion, more like “Oh boy, here we go again.”    
- Better research can use full population, account for quality of interactions, lack of evidence quality measure, do these findings hold up across different modes of conviction?, how generalizable is this work?, how would this change for private defense attorneys?, take into account attitudes and biases of courtroom actors, further account for race/gender of the actors.
