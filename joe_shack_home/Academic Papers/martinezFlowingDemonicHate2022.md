## Abstract
Prior research has found that different aspects of religion (such as service attendance and fundamentalism) have significant and varying effects on public attitudes about immigration. We identify an important but understudied aspect of how religion connects to immigration attitudes: beliefs about the reality of supernatural evil (e.g., Satan, hell, and demons). Using a national sample of Americans, we find that greater belief in supernatural evil is a strong and consistent predictor of more restrictive views of immigration, even after controlling for other dimensions of religiosity, sociodemographics, and political characteristics. Overall, beliefs about religious evil are the aspect of religion with the strongest connection to views of immigration. Consequently, consideration of religious evil is integral to understanding how religion influences public attitudes about immigration.
## Theory
1. Belief in supernatural evil amplifies in-group/out-group distinctions as it creates a stronger distinction between good and evil. Stems from the [[spiritual warfare perspective]].
2. Prior research has found somewhat conflicting results regarding the effects of religion on immigration attitudes. Frequency of service attendance has been found to be correlated with more pro-immigration attitudes while association with Christian nationalism has been found to be associated with anti-immigration attitudes.
3. Authors argue that through the use of [[moral panic]], immigration has been linked to criminality and racial/ethnic invasion. Paired with the spiritual warfare perspective, individuals are more primed to see social/moral issues in black and white. This creates strong in-groups and out-groups, and immigrants typically become tied to the out-group.
## Hypothesis + Results
1. Belief in supernatural evil will be correlated with favoring stricter immigration policy.
	1. [[Goodman and Kruskal's gamma]] (a way of calculating the correlation between two ordinal variables) were moderately high (0.3 and 0.34).
	2. Basically find a strong and statistically significant relationship.
	3. One interesting exploratory finding was that social trust in strangers was correlated with more pro-immigration attitudes and was not explained away by belief in supernatural evil (contrary to other studies).

## Data
* Used Wave IV of the [[Baylor Religion Survey]] from 2014.
### Dependent variables
1. We should restrict and control people coming into our country more than we do now (4-point scale).
2. Thinking about government spending, do you think that the federal government spends too much, about the right amount, or too little on patrolling and controlling our borders? (3-point scale).
### Independent variables
1. [[Belief in supernatural evil scale]] developed using the BRS. [[Cronbach's Alpha]] of 0.945.
	1. In three separate questions, respondents were asked if they believed that the following existed: the devil/Satan, Hell, and demons. Each question provided the following four responses: “absolutely not,” “probably not,” “probably,” and “absolutely.”
### Controls
1. **Religious Affiliation**: Use the [[RELTRAD]] religious typology. The categories are: Evangelical Protestant, Black Protestant, Mainline Protestant, Catholic, Jewish, Other, or None. It is determined based on the name and location of the congregation the respondent attends as well as their stated denomination.
2. **Congregational Attendance**: never, less than once a year, once or twice a year, several times a year, once a month, 2–3 times a month, about weekly, weekly, and several times a week (8-point ordinal scale).
3. **Religious salience**: not religious (1), slightly religious (2), moderately religious (3), and very religious (4)
4. **Biblical literalism**: (0/1) Does the Bible mean exactly what it says and it should be taken literally work-for-word on all subjects?
5. **Political party**: 1 indicates strong Republican, 4 indicates independent, and 7 indicates strong Democrat (7-point scale).
6. **Tea Party Association**: I assume this is a binary variable.
7. **Sex**: Male/Female
8. **Citizen status**: Yes/no
9. **Marital Status**: Yes/no
10. **Region**: South/Not the South
11. **Race/ethnicity**: White, Black, Hispanic, Asian, Other, Multiracial.
12. **Age**: Continuous
13. **Income**: 7-point scale.
14. **Education**: 7-point scale.
15. **Economic anxiety**: 0/1, "Did someone in the household lose a job because of the bad economy in the past 5 years?"
16. **Social trust in people**: How much do you trust people in general? 4-point scale ranging from not at all to a lot.
17. **Social trust in strangers**: How much do you trust strangers? 4-point scale ranging from not at all to a lot.
## Robustness checks
1. Conducted [[path analysis]] and found most of the effects for religious identification can be explained by differential rates of belief in supernatural evil. However, Catholic identification had a larger direct effect than indirect effect mediated by belief in supernatural evil.
2. Interacted belief in supernatural evil with political affiliation. They did not find any significant associations with government spending. However, they did find the effect of believing in supernatural evil was stronger for Democrats and served to attenuate political differences (which is very strange).
