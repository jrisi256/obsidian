## Abstract

Max Weber's Economy and Society is the greatest sociological treatise written in this century. Published posthumously in Germany in the early 1920's, it has become a constitutive part of the modern sociological imagination. Economy and Society was the first strictly empirical comparison of social structures and normative orders in world-historical depth, containing the famous chapters on social action, religion, law, bureaucracy, charisma, the city, and the political community with its dimensions of class, status and power. Economy and Status is Weber's only major treatise for an educated general public. It was meant to be a broad introduction, but in its own way it is the most demanding textbook yet written by a sociologist. The precision of its definitions, the complexity of its typologies and the wealth of its historical content make the work a continuous challenge at several levels of comprehension: for the advanced undergraduate who gropes for his sense of society, for the graduate student who must develop his own analytical skills, and for the scholar who must match wits with Weber. When the long-awaited first complete English edition of Economy and Society was published in 1968, Arthur Stinchcombe wrote in the American Journal of Sociology: "My answer to the question of whether people should still start their sociological intellectual biographies with Economy and Society is yes." Reinhard Bendix noted in the American Sociological Review that the "publication of a compete English edition of Weber's most systematic work *represents* the culmination of a cultural transmission to the American setting... It will be a study-guide and compendium for years to come for all those interested in historical sociology and comparative study." In a lengthy introduction, Guenther Roth traces the intellectual prehistory of Economy and Society, the gradual emergence of its dominant themes and the nature of its internal logic. Mr. Roth is a Professor of Sociology at Columbia University. Mr. Wittich heads an economic research group at the United Nations.
## Notes
1. Charismatic authority is something given to the leader or something they are endowed with (from the point of view of their followers, some would argue). It cannot be learned or earned. It is seen as something coming from divine origin.
2. Those subject to your authority must recognize you as having charisma. Otherwise you simply don't have it, or the ruled can revoke their recognition. Once your charismatic authority is acknowledged, those under its sway follow it unquestioningly. If proof of your authority (i.e., success) eludes the leader for too long, if their magical powers have disappeared or their God had abandoned them, if they stop benefiting their followers, their charismatic authority will disappear. For this reason, charisma is fragile and constantly depends on their power being demonstrated.
3. Charismatic leaders don't create formal bureaucratic hierarchies to administer their authority. Rules are created based on their whims and in the moment feelings and reasoning.
4. Charisma is revolutionary in the sense it does not respect the rules or traditions which came before. There are no formal rules really only belief in the charismatic ruler.
	1. Contrast this with bureaucratic rationalization which is also revolutionary but because it changes the rules through technical or goal-oriented processes which are objective (i.e., not subject to individual subjective discretion). These changes people *from without* while charismatic leadership changes people *from within*.
5. While similar in some ways, charisma and tradition are fundamentally different. Tradition has the ruled follow its rules because that is the way it has always been. Under charisma, the ruled follow the rules because they fundamentally believe in the leader.
6. Truly charismatic communities are rare and are generally communistic. See monastic communities. Everyday wants and needs are subjugated to the ruler. Rigid hierarchies, bureaucracy, and rules are not as important (although I do not know how true this is).
7. Charisma is impossible to sustain in the long-term and eventually morphs into tradition out of necessity. Charisma cannot sustain people in everyday life.
